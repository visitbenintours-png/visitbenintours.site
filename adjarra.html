Visit Adjarra: Black River, Crafts & Local Culture

Southeast of Porto-Novo, the dark, still waters of the Rivière Noire wind through raffia palms and artisan villages — a quiet, little-visited side of Bénin known for basketry, drums, and traditional sodabi palm spirit.

The Black River of Adjarra

The Rivière Noire — Black River — takes its name from the dark tone of its waters as it winds quietly through the Ouémé department in southeastern Bénin, connecting the town of Adjarra toward Porto-Novo as a tributary of the Ouémé River. Bordered by raffia palms and fertile, rural land, it has long been used by local communities for transport, fishing and agriculture — and has more recently become a small but growing tourist attraction, explored by traditional pirogue.

The river moves so slowly that the pirogue barely needs steering — just the quiet dip of a single pole, raffia palms leaning in from both banks, and village life continuing exactly as it has for generations.

Adjarra: The City of Drums

Adjarra is officially known in Bénin as the "Cité des Tambours" — City of Drums — for its long tradition of drum-making and musical instrument craftsmanship. The market for tam-tams and other traditional instruments remains one of the commune's cultural highlights, alongside its six historic arrondissements.

Basketry

Raffia basketry is one of Adjarra's best-known crafts, using palm and reed harvested locally along the river. Skilled weavers produce baskets, mats and woven objects using techniques passed down through generations of artisan families.

Traditional Crafts

Beyond basketry and drums, Adjarra's artisans work in terracotta pottery, wood carving and forged metalwork, alongside wooden masks used in Vodun ceremonies — traditional religion remains the majority faith in the commune, and Vodun festivities are among its most important cultural celebrations.

Local Workshops

A visit here pairs a peaceful river excursion with genuine encounters with basket-weavers, drum-makers and sodabi distillers, in a part of Bénin that sees far fewer visitors than Ganvié or Ouidah. Most workshops welcome visitors to watch the process and buy directly from the artisans.

Things to Do in Adjarra

🛶 Black River Pirogue Trip

A calm, traditional canoe excursion along the Rivière Noire between Adjarra and Avrankou, through raffia palm groves.

🧺 Raffia Basketry & Crafts

Adjarra is renowned for its raffia weaving, wood carving and traditional drum-making workshops.

🥃 Sodabi Distillation

Visit a local producer of sodabi, Bénin's traditional palm-wine spirit, and see the full distillation process.

🎭 Vodun Heritage

Adjarra's artisans also produce ceremonial masks and objects, offering insight into local Vodun traditions.

FAQ — Adjarra & the Black River

Where exactly is the Black River?
It flows through the Ouémé department in southeastern Bénin, linking the town of Adjarra toward Porto-Novo, and is a tributary of the larger Ouémé River.
Can Adjarra be combined with Porto-Novo?
Yes — Adjarra sits close to Porto-Novo, making it easy to combine a Black River pirogue trip with a visit to Bénin's historic capital in the same day.
What souvenirs can I find in Adjarra?
Raffia baskets, hand-carved wooden objects, traditional drums and terracotta pottery are all made locally by Adjarra's artisan community.
