Is Bénin Safe for Tourists? An Honest Answer

A clear, honest look at safety in Bénin — what official advisories actually say, which areas to avoid, and what the real risk picture looks like for the destinations most travellers visit.

We'd rather give you an accurate, slightly uncomfortable answer than a reassuring one that isn't true. Here is what the official sources actually say, as of early 2026.

The short version

Southern Bénin — Cotonou, Ganvié, Ouidah, Porto-Novo, Abomey, Grand-Popo, and the coastal region where the overwhelming majority of visitors spend their trip — carries a similar risk profile to most West African capitals and tourist regions: petty theft and opportunistic crime are the main concerns, not terrorism or kidnapping. This is the part of the country nearly all of our tours operate in.

The northern border regions are a different picture, and we won't pretend otherwise. Both the U.S. State Department and the UK Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office currently advise against travel to areas near the borders with Burkina Faso and Niger, citing terrorism and kidnapping risk linked to Sahel-based armed groups. This advisory explicitly includes Pendjari National Park and the areas immediately surrounding it, along with the W National Park and a strip of territory near the northern and northeastern borders.

What this means in practice

We take this seriously, and so should you. Before booking or travelling to northern Bénin — including a Pendjari safari — check the current advisory for your own nationality directly:

  • U.S. citizens: travel.state.gov
  • UK nationals: gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice
  • Other nationalities: check your own government's foreign ministry travel advice page

Advisories change, sometimes quickly, and they can differ meaningfully between neighbouring countries or even between one part of a region and another. We do not book or recommend travel to any area currently under a "do not travel" advisory, and we monitor these sources before confirming any northern itinerary.

Southern Bénin: the realistic picture

For the coastal south — where Ganvié, Ouidah, Porto-Novo, Cotonou, Abomey and Grand-Popo are located — the general safety concerns are the ones common to most travel in the region: bag snatching, opportunistic theft, and the usual caution around walking alone at night or displaying valuables. Serious violent crime targeting tourists is uncommon. Beninese people are, by near-universal traveller account, exceptionally welcoming.

Practical safety habits we recommend

  • Use a money belt or keep valuables in your accommodation's safe rather than on you
  • Avoid walking alone late at night, particularly in unfamiliar areas of Cotonou
  • Keep car doors locked and windows up in traffic
  • Don't display expensive jewellery or camera gear unnecessarily
  • Travel with a reputable local guide, particularly outside major towns
  • Register with your embassy's travel programme if one is available for your nationality
  • Buy travel insurance that covers your actual itinerary and activities

Our approach

Every one of our guides is local, and our itineraries are built around the regions where we can genuinely stand behind your safety. If you're specifically interested in a northern safari, ask us directly — we'll give you the current, honest picture for your dates and nationality rather than a generic yes.
