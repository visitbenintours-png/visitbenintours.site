Most nationalities need a visa to enter Bénin, and since 2018 this is handled through an online e-visa system rather than a stamp from an embassy.

## Do you need a visa?

Most visitors do. Citizens of ECOWAS member states (the West African regional bloc) generally benefit from visa-free entry under regional agreements. Some other nationalities and specific circumstances may also be exempt. Because requirements can change and vary by nationality, always check your specific eligibility before you travel rather than assuming.

## How the e-visa works

Bénin's e-visa is applied for entirely online before you travel. In broad terms:

- You submit an application with your passport details and travel information
- Processing is typically a few working days, though faster paid options often exist
- Once approved, you receive an electronic visa to print and carry with your passport
- The visa is checked and your entry stamped on arrival, generally at Cotonou's airport

Visa types are usually offered as single-entry (around 30 days) or multiple-entry (30 or 90 days), depending on your travel plans.

## What you'll need

- A passport valid for at least 6 months beyond your arrival date
- At least one blank page in your passport
- A return or onward travel ticket
- Proof of sufficient funds for your stay, in some cases
- A yellow fever vaccination certificate — commonly required for entry into Bénin and worth sorting out well ahead of travel

## A word of caution on where you apply

Search for "Bénin visa" and you'll find a long list of third-party agencies, some charging significantly more than the official government fee for the same service. If you're comfortable navigating it yourself, applying directly through Bénin's official government visa portal is the most cost-effective route. If you'd rather not deal with it, a reputable, clearly-labelled visa service can help — just be aware of the markup you're paying for convenience.

## When to apply

Apply well ahead of your trip rather than at the last minute. Processing is usually quick, but leaving a buffer avoids stress if any part of your application needs a correction.
