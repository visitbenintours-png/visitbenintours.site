These are the two most-booked day trips from Cotonou, and they could not be more different from each other. Here's how to think about the choice.

## Ganvié, in short

A stilt village of 30,000 people built entirely on Lake Nokoué, reached by private pirogue. The experience is visual, peaceful, and centred on daily life — a floating market, children paddling to school, fishermen working the water. Best at sunrise. <a href="/ganvie.html">Full Ganvié guide →</a>

## Ouidah, in short

A town carrying the weight of history — the Route des Esclaves, the Door of No Return, and the living heart of Vodun spiritual practice, including the Python Temple. The experience is emotional, historical, and reflective rather than scenic. <a href="/ouidah.html">Full Ouidah guide →</a>

## If you only have time for one

**Choose Ganvié if** you want a lighter, visually stunning morning — especially if travelling with children, or if this is meant to be a relaxed introduction to Bénin rather than a heavy first day.

**Choose Ouidah if** history and culture are the primary reason you're visiting Bénin, particularly if you have a personal or ancestral connection to the transatlantic slave trade, or a serious interest in Vodun as a living spiritual tradition.

## The good news: you probably don't have to choose

Ganvié and Ouidah are close enough to Cotonou, and to each other, that combining both in a single well-planned day — or splitting them across two consecutive mornings — is entirely realistic. It's one of our most-booked combinations for exactly this reason. <a href="/tours-2-days.html">See our Ganvié + Ouidah 2-day circuit →</a>

## Our honest recommendation

If your schedule allows two separate outings, do both — they genuinely don't compete with each other, they complement each other. If you truly have only one morning in Bénin, let your own travel priorities decide: scenery and gentle culture, or history and depth.
