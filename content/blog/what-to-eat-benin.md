Beninese food doesn't get the international attention of some of its neighbours, but it's worth seeking out — built around maize, cassava, fresh fish, and a genuine love of chilli.

## Akassa

A fermented corn dough, steamed in leaves, with a mild sour tang from the fermentation. It's a staple starch served alongside sauces and grilled fish, sold at roadside stands across the country — one of the most common everyday foods you'll encounter.

## Wagasi

A firm, mild cheese made from cow's milk, traditionally by Fulani communities in northern Bénin. It holds its shape when fried or grilled, making it a popular addition to street food and a genuinely distinctive regional specialty for a part of the world not typically known for cheese.

## Pâte and sauces

Pâte — a thick dough made from maize, cassava or yam flour — is the everyday base of most Beninese meals, eaten by hand and dipped into a sauce. Sauces vary widely: peanut (arachide), tomato-based, or built around okra and leafy greens, often with fish, chicken or goat.

## Grilled fish

Along the Atlantic coast — Cotonou, Grand-Popo, Ouidah — freshly grilled fish is everywhere, usually simply seasoned and served with a side of pâte or rice. It's consistently one of the best, freshest meals available in the country.

## Akara

Deep-fried bean fritters, crisp outside and soft within, sold hot from street stalls — a common breakfast or snack food, and a good introduction to Beninese street food if you're easing in.

## Sodabi

Bénin's traditional distilled palm wine spirit, strong and distinctive, produced locally including around Adjarra. Worth trying in moderation, and genuinely interesting to see distilled if you visit a producer.

## Fresh fruit

Mango, pineapple, papaya and citrus are widely available and excellent, particularly in season — a reliable, refreshing option when you want something simple.

## A practical note

Street food in Bénin is generally safe when it's freshly cooked in front of you and served hot. Use common sense: busy stalls with high turnover are usually a good sign, and bottled or treated water is the safer choice throughout your trip.
